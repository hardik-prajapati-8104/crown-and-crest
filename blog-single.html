A 10-Step Checklist for Planning a Corporate Event in Dubai

From objectives to post-event review, the steps that keep a corporate event on time, on budget and on message.

CorporateCrown & Crest Editorial4 min read

A corporate event is a business tool: it should move a metric, strengthen a relationship or launch a message. This checklist keeps the planning focused on outcomes.

1. Define the objective

Write one sentence describing what success looks like: leads generated, partners thanked, a product understood. Every later decision should serve it.

2. Set the budget and contingency

Agree the total budget early and hold back 10–15% for the unexpected. Itemise venue, production, catering, talent, marketing and staffing.

3. Choose the date and venue

Check public holidays, religious observances, competing industry events and peak-season availability. Visit the venue at the same time of day as the event.

4. Build the guest experience

Map the journey from invitation to departure: registration, welcome, flow, seating, content and farewell. Remove friction at every step.

5. Brief suppliers early

Catering, AV, décor, talent and transport should all work from one run-sheet. Early briefing reduces cost and mistakes.

6. Plan for permits and compliance

Entertainment, music, lighting effects and outdoor set-ups may require approvals. Confirm requirements with your venue, planner and the relevant authorities well in advance.

7. Rehearse the programme

Run speeches, cues, audio-visual content and transitions at least once. Rehearsal is the cheapest insurance an event can buy.

8. Prepare a contingency plan

Weather, late speakers, technical failures and guest surges are predictable. Decide the response before the day.

9. Manage the day on site

Appoint one decision-maker, brief the team before doors open and keep a clear communication channel for every supplier.

10. Measure and debrief

Collect feedback, compare against the original objective and record what to repeat or improve next time.

Events are won in preparation. By the time guests arrive, the experience should already be decided.
